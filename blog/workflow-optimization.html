WORKFLOW DESIGN

When a NetSuite workflow becomes too complex

Complexity is not measured only by the number of states. The real question is whether someone can still understand, test, and safely change the business logic.

Start with the business policy

Before changing the workflow, document who should approve, under which conditions, in which order, and what should happen when data changes. Many technical problems begin because the configured workflow no longer matches a clearly stated policy.

Look for repeated decisions

Repeated actions, duplicated conditions, parallel transitions, and state-specific exceptions make routing harder to explain. A good redesign consolidates common decisions and keeps each state responsible for one clear purpose.

A workflow should make the approval policy visible—not hide it inside a maze of transitions.

Know when SuiteScript is appropriate

Workflow is useful for readable routing and simple actions. SuiteScript becomes appropriate when validation, data preparation, reusable calculation, high-volume processing, or complex branching is easier to test and maintain in code.

Test the paths, not only the happy path

  • Standard approval and rejection
  • Threshold and subsidiary variations
  • Missing or changed approver data
  • Resubmission after editing
  • Delegation and inactive employees
  • Production deployment and rollback

The goal is supportability

The best workflow is not the one with the fewest states. It is the one that accurately enforces the policy, performs reliably, and can be understood by the next person who needs to support it.
